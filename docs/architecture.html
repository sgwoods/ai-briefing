Architecture

AI Briefing is a daily AI briefing for early-stage investors: a written edition, a ~10-minute podcast episode with the day's deep dive, a static site and podcast feed, and a subscriber email. One machine runs the whole pipeline each morning, mostly with local models. Nothing reaches readers until a release gate has passed.

This page describes the design as of v0.2 beta. Release notes: CHANGELOG.md and the site's release notes. Project page: sgwoods.github.io/public/ai-briefing.html.

Design principles

  • Evidence over volume. Sources are weighed by how they know things (primary, reporting, analysis, opinion, aggregator, social), not by how many repeat a claim. A Must read needs primary or reporting evidence.
  • Check before sending. Figures are traced to their sources and searched for before they are cut. Big claims get a critical read. Thin items have their article read.
  • Local first. A local model (Ollama or LM Studio) handles most stages, Claude the critical pass and editing. Audio is local Kokoro TTS. Free and local tools come before paid services.
  • Fail safe, not silent. An outbound step that can't pass the gate holds the work and tells the owner what to do. It never sends half-checked output.
  • Private by construction. Personal values live in one private file, not in code. Public outputs are scanned for private terms before they leave.
  • Data, never instructions. Everything fetched (posts, newsletters, replies, pages) is treated as content to assess, never as commands.

Components

  • Models (llm.py): one small interface over a local OpenAI-compatible server, Claude via the CLI, or Claude via the API. Each stage picks its backend in config.
  • Editorial lens (perspective.py): a hand-written perspective file (people, topics, scepticism) steers filtering and the editor's view. Code reads it and never rewrites it.
  • Jev in the shadow (jev.py): TypeSafe's structured-decision model is asked keep/drop for every item the local filter judged, and the two answers are logged side by side (data/state/jev-shadow.jsonl) with a daily token budget; nothing it says changes the edition until a week's agreement says it should (./np jev report). Public fetched items only; the key is in the Keychain; off in staging.
  • The advance deep dive (deepdive.draft_tomorrow, apply_draft_feedback; launchd agent advance, 15:30): tomorrow's deep dive is written and checked in the afternoon and saved to Drive as a draft with "Steven's take" and "Notes for the editor" sections. The morning run reads the Doc back: the take goes in; notes make the writer revise the draft (checked again, the take kept); then it is voiced and shipped with the edition.
  • The schedule (schedule_page.py): the day's timetable as a table, built from config: what is produced, when, where it goes, and the window for Steven's feedback on each piece and how it is picked up (dashboard /schedule, ./np schedule).
  • The morning post (slackpost.py, card.py): when the email goes out, one branded post in the team channel from the app's bot user: the day's leads, one of Steven's published takes, the deep dive, the links, and the day's card (a PNG in the newsletter's colours, drawn with Pillow and the Inter files in assets/fonts). Gated like every outbound step; once a day; the Slack reader ignores bot posts.
  • Drive copies and takes from the Docs (drivedocs.py): the edition and deep dive are written to the Briefings folder as Google Docs. Steven's edits to their takes sections (alone or with Claude in the Doc) are read back every 20 minutes and applied like any other take, through the same privacy check; an edited deep-dive take re-records the deep dive.
  • House views (houseviews.py): a second hand-written file, house_views.md, holds the views Our read takes positions under and Steven's dated theses. Each Must read's Our read names its view; the lead story adds the case for, the case against, the deciding question and our lean. Every position goes to data/positions/ledger.jsonl with what would change our mind and a check date. On Sundays the calls that are due are scored against what we have reported since (right, wrong, mixed; "too early" gets more time), and the site's Calls we made page lists them.
  • Accuracy audit (audit.py): after the edition ships, about five of its claims are checked again against their own sources, and the consistency flags for the day are counted. Partly right or contradicted claims send Steven one alert; the site's Accuracy page shows the daily counts and every correction (shipped.py). The same module runs the checks before sending (audit.presend) and before a deep dive is recorded (audit.check_sections); ./np audit deepdive DAY is a dry run on a recorded dive. Details: Fact checking 4d and 4e.
  • The top of the edition (textutil.legacy_top): with briefing.top_format: merged one 5-bullet "Today in 30 seconds" with the diligence question replaces the day in brief and Top of mind; every reader of the markdown sees it in the classic shape through one function.
  • Tuning (tuning.py, profiles/): named presets for lengths, sections and tone, edited from the dashboard.
  • Dashboard (dashboard/): a local Flask app on 127.0.0.1 for sources, watchlist, tuning, takes, feedback, runs, quality and control. It's never exposed to the internet.
  • Services (service.py): launchd agents for the daily run, the evening preflight and the dashboard.
  • Secrets (vault.py): macOS Keychain first, then .env (gitignored, mode 600).

Grounding and fact checking

Every claim a reader sees must trace back to text the pipeline holds: the archived item, the article the reader fetched, or a page found by search. The checks run in layers: ground, judge, check every figure and quote, check the first lines against the body, then the privacy gate. After shipping, editions change only through visible corrections. Unverifiable sentences are cut and the edition still goes. Only privacy blocks. The full list, with the reasons behind each layer, is in Fact checking.

Steven's takes

From v0.2 the owner can add his own view to the day's edition, verbatim and labelled "Steven's take" (takes.py, docs/plans/my-takes.md). Once the edition is written, a private email lists numbered slots: 0 for the day overall, T for Top of mind, and 1 to N for each Must-read story. The email carries the whole edition, with an "Add your take" button after each section that opens a reply with the section's number filled in (mail clients strip real text boxes), and a private "Type your takes on one page" link. The day's site page isn't published until the cutoff, so that page is served by the mail script instead (v0.2.10): the pipeline uploads the held edition with a box after each section and one Submit (setreview, key only), and the script shows it only with his signed token (scope t, two days). The boxes post to the script, which stores the takes for the pipeline to read (the review / takes / takes_pull actions in the Apps Script). After publishing, the site page shows the same boxes with his token (takes.js). He replies from his phone ("1: ..."), or posts in his team's Slack channel with the story's link (his own words only; nobody else's text is ever kept). The edition waits until the cutoff (09:15) or a "done" reply, then:

  • each take is checked for private names, the owner's addresses, personal links, disclosure phrases and the confidential marker; one that fails is held back and listed to him, never published;
  • the clean takes go into one store (data/commentary/&lt;day>.json, the same store the dashboard writes), which the site, the email and the mail-out read;
  • the host reads each take verbatim after its story ("Here's Steven's take"), and only the briefing audio is re-rendered; the briefing text and its fact checks don't change;
  • raw replies, held takes and what was published are kept in the private archive.

Takes are opinion: they sit outside the sourced claims and the fact check, and no model rewrites them. A crash or mailbox failure never blocks the edition; it ships without takes. Later retries see a run that is still waiting and leave it alone.

The weekly critic

Every Saturday the briefing is scored against the latest episode or issue of ten of the most popular tech and AI shows, with a fixed ten-part rubric weighted for an investor daily: content selection, accuracy, depth, timeliness, structure, opinion, distinctiveness, writing, tone and production. A private email gives the owner the score and rank, charts by characteristic against the field, the trend week by week and ten ranked suggestions with options; the owner picks what to build that week (critic.py). Method, rubric, limits and the latest results: The critic.

Data and storage

  • Archive: append-only files under data/ (items, analyses, stories, runs). Nothing deletes or rewrites history.
  • Editions: briefings/&lt;day>.md, the audio and the deep dives. The site is rebuilt from these each publish.
  • Editorial inputs: the perspective, source lists, newsletter rules, watchlist, source profiles and pronunciations. They're hand-edited or edited from the dashboard.
  • People: subscribers live in a Google Sheet behind the mailer. Feedback is stored with a hashed sender id and a first name only.
  • State: seen-message lists, release approvals, alerts. Everything private is committed to a private repository and backed up after each run.

Privacy and security

  • Release gate (release.py): every outbound action (site, email, Gmail label writes, backups, hub, public code) calls require(). It re-runs the tests, the secret scan and the invariants when code changes, and needs the owner's approval of security-sensitive files. Invariants include:
  • mailbox access only through a least-privilege guard;
  • no private author notes in any prompt;
  • outbound URLs from content checked against an allowlist (netguard.py);
  • every outbound function behind the gate.
  • Mailbox (mailguard.py): one IMAP wrapper. Each purpose may open only its own labels, read-only except for adding labels.
  • Scans: the site, every email and every public export are scanned for private names, the owner's addresses, private links and trackers before they leave.
  • Owner details: names, addresses, organization and bio live in owner.yaml, which is never public. Code defaults are neutral placeholders.
  • Staging (env.py): a development checkout can't reach readers. The gate refuses every outbound action there, and the mailer only sends review copies to the owner, marked [STAGING].

Shipped editions don't break

Once a day's email has gone out, that day is shipped (shipped.py). Every production publish is checked against what readers already have. Shipped pages and deep dives must still be there, a shipped edition's text changes only through a visible correction, shipped audio inside the retention window keeps its URL (a remaster adds a file and keeps the old one), and the feeds can't shrink. After the push, the newest shipped pages, both feeds and the latest MP3 must answer, or the previous site is put back and the email waits. A failed publish never sends the email.

Releases

Production runs a tagged release (VERSION, CHANGELOG.md). Changes are built on a development branch whose checkout is in staging mode. They're verified with a full staging run compared against production, tagged as a release candidate, reviewed, and promoted in a safe window with a single approval. The next morning's run is the canary, and a rollback returns to the previous, already-approved release.

From v0.2.33 a release goes out through the ship job in one of two lanes: the slot lane, once per weekday from 13:00 (release.slot), or the fix lane, for something broken, any time in the release window (08:30–20:00, or earlier once the edition is out). Every ship first soaks: today's edition is rebuilt with the dev code and compared with production, and a compare that needs review stops the ship unless the owner accepts it. Rules and approvals: docs/security/RELEASE.md. Editorial content (sources, watchlist, perspective, takes) isn't code and is edited in production at any time.

Command Where What

Run queue

From v0.1.12 the owner can run releases and fixes from the Claude chat instead of a terminal (runner.py, docs/plans/run-queue.md). Claude writes a job file naming a recipe and its arguments. A launchd agent on the Mac runs only allowlisted recipes, with validated arguments, as argument lists rather than shell text. There are three tiers:

Tier Recipes Runs when
auto tests, sync, preview, compare, doctor, gate, verify, consistency, critic-email, takes-test, takes-check, takes-sync, drive-save, mailer-status, audio-check, audit, audit-deepdive, x-probe, advance, and (from v0.2.37) remaster and deepdive-rebuild at once
chat correct, publish, restore, takes-done, takes-apply, send-held, source-add, mailer-setup the job quotes the owner's yes from the chat
mac ship, release, rollback, approve, install-hooks, phone-setup, ntfy-setup, the public code push, deploy-mailer for ship and release only (v0.2.37), the owner's yes quoted from the chat when the task starts, once tests and the soak pass; otherwise the owner clicks Approve in a dialog on the Mac, or on his phone (below) (also early, while the morning run is still going: the job then runs as soon as the edition is out); a ship's Approve also covers the public code export and the mail-script deploy

The click grants presence in memory for that job's approved steps only (presence.py). Apart from the quoted chat yes for ship and release (which rests on Claude quoting the owner faithfully, as the chat tier does), no file or setting can stand in for it: an early click is kept as a signature over that job, made with a key in the Mac's Keychain (which the VM and Claude can't read), used once and only that day. Jobs that change production wait out the morning run, which ends when the day's edition has gone out. Unanswered approvals lapse at 20:00.

Phone approvals (v0.2.22): once the phone-setup job has stored an approval passphrase in the Keychain (typed into a Mac dialog), each mac-tier job also emails the owner a one-time link to the mail script's approve page, good for that job and that day. He types the passphrase there and taps Approve or Reject. The page sends only a proof (HMAC of the answer and job id under PBKDF2 of the passphrase, salted with the link's nonce), and the runner checks it against the Keychain on its next tick. Neither the mail script's records nor the email can stand in for it, so Claude, which can read both, still can't approve. The Mac dialog still appears, for 90 seconds, so the runner keeps ticking while it waits.

Phone notifications (ntfy.py, v0.2.24): the preferred phone path. The Mac publishes to a private ntfy topic (a random name kept only in its Keychain; the phone's ntfy app subscribes once). An approval is a notification with Approve and Reject buttons; a tap posts the job's one-time token to a second private topic the runner polls, so no passphrase is typed. Nudges (the takes page is ready, the evening note is out, a run needs attention) open the owner pages in one tap, with owner links minted the way the mail script mints them. Set up with the ntfy-setup job (a Mac Approve), then ./np ntfy test. The runner refuses to work if production code differs from the release. Release invariant 10 checks the recipes and the runner's code. Results go to data/queue/done/ and logs/runner-&lt;id>.txt. ./np runner status lists the queue.

Keeping these docs current

  • On every release (it blocks): the drift tests in the release gate check that this page names the running version, that every module it names exists, that every public module is described in a hand-written doc, and that every diagram is used. Generated sections (current facts, component reference) are rebuilt at each publish, and the site's docs are rebuilt with them.
  • At the decision: the release approval dialog and the release output list the modules that changed while no doc describing them did ("docs to review"). The list is saved to data/state/docs-impact.json.
  • Afterwards (automatic): a weekly docs refresh reads that list and the week's changes, updates the docs and diagrams on the development branch, and leaves them for the next release. Docs changes ship through releases like code.

Operations

  • Schedule: early morning with two retries. A retry skips work already done. When takes are on, the edition is built early and held until the cutoff (09:15) or the owner's "done"; retries at 09:30, 10:30 and 12:00 send a held edition if the Mac was offline at the cutoff. An evening preflight checks that tomorrow's release is approved.
  • Hearing what was published (v0.2.17): after the email, the pipeline reads the public podcast feed, downloads the day's episode, transcribes it locally with Whisper and compares it with the script it was voiced from (listen.py). A section that wasn't heard (low coverage) sends one action-needed email. The transcript is kept in data/transcripts/ and published with the next publish: a WebVTT file next to the episode, a podcast:transcript tag in the feed and a link on the day's page. ./np audio-check [DAY] (run queue: audio-check).
  • Copies in Drive (v0.2.11): the edition and the deep dive are also written as Google Docs to the mailer account's Briefings folder in Drive, shared with the owner, so he (and Claude, through Drive) can read them anywhere and give feedback. At the pre-release takes email: &lt;day> · AI Briefing (draft, held for your takes) · &lt;lead> and &lt;day> · Deep dive · &lt;title>, linked from that email. Once published, the draft is renamed &lt;day> · AI Briefing · &lt;lead> and rewritten with the owner's takes, and the deep dive doc is rewritten. The mail script writes them (savedoc, key only, drivedocs.py); a rerun rewrites the same doc and nothing is deleted. ./np drive save [DAY] [draft] (run queue: drive-save) writes them by hand. A Drive failure never holds the edition.
  • When something needs the owner: an action-needed email states the issue and the fix. Approving re-sends what was held back.
  • Outside view: a cloud watchdog checks the public site after the send window, and the project hub compares the exported release with the public code.
  • Commands: ./np help. Every command saves its full output under logs/.
